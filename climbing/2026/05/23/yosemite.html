Yosemite 2026

Created on May 23, 2026

My second season in Yosemite was…interesting! I stayed in Camp 4 for three weeks and had a different climbing partner each week. I packed a lot of climbing in, which I loved. However the vibes in the Valley ate at me. The tourons, while easy to avoid once you’re actually on a route, were a part of my existence every day. For me climbing is about the whole experience. Your partner. The setting. The movement. The protection. The weather. The friction. Etc. Yosemite, with all the tourists, climbers, Starbucks, and…well, again, the freaking tourists (!) is just not my ideal rock climbing experience.

Many of the veterans know this. They park their vans at El Cap and grocery shop in Mariposa. They exist almost completely apart from the tourists. Meanwhile, living in Camp 4 in 2026, you simply cannot escape the madness.

Ultimately the vibe I love best for rock climbing is what led me to live in Leavenworth; abundant solitude and true wilderness. Every day at home I choose my own scene, and I love days bouldering with friends just as much as days rope soloing at Index. Everyone’s different, but I originally left California for a reason and I am not in a hurry to go back to the inescapable crowds. I think my future climbing trips will trend more towards crowd avoidance. The Fall Yosemite season, I hear, might be more my speed.

Climbs This year I climbed a bunch of moderate classics like the Nutcracker, Kor Beck, Absolutely Free, Central Pillar, Serenity Sons, Mr Natural, Bishops Terrace, and The Surprise. I also did some wall climbing — Skull Queen (in a day), East Buttress of El Cap (not technically a wall 😉), and bailing off the Nose after hauling to / sleeping on Sickle (more below…). And last-but-not-least I got on some really great single pitch and modern routes — Voyager, Yin Yang, Crack-A-Go-Go, Mack Daddy, Center of the Universe. A very diverse, informative, and exhausting trip!


Getting There

We've been robbed! Lol. Crazy to pack up almost every piece of gear you own for one trip. If you're gonna do it for anywhere it's THE Valley!

Volcano tour! Adams and Hood.

Loukomades from the amazing food truck in Redmond and Shasta! My stop for the night.

Week 1

Made it! After picking Ben up from the Fresno airport we quickly ran up the Nutcracker — we probably should have simuled the thing since we climbed it in just over an hour while pitching it out! Beware — the base had BY FAR the worst mosquitos in the Valley.

We visited Cookie Cliff and Mecca on our first day, climbed a new E Sloan route "Into the Wild / Yose University" on a rainy day, and then biked up valley to do Skull Queen in a day the following morning! Ben pictured cruising up the Kor Roof.

More shots of Skull Queen in a day.

A trip up the Voyager with Ben. I did not quite send, but led most of the cruxes (odds) and was very close! I'll get it next time no problem.


Week 2

Jack has arrived! And just like the Nutcracker with Ben, I figured we'd cruise up Absolutely Free in the last 1-2 hours of daylight and walk off Middle Brother in 10 minutes. Easy right?

Holy shit I could not have been more wrong! This was my first time getting epicked. I did not read any beta on the descent in advance — turns out it all says "do not try this at night!" Also, unbeknownst to me, my hastily-packed headlamp had fallen off my harness as I took my first step on the approach! We found it next to my backpack afterwards.

In the end we managed to find our way down at around 1am. Not my proudest moment, but kind of fun in a way...type 3 fun at least!

The last pic above is Jack leading up Central Pillar the next day.

A sweet trip up the Regular Route on Higher Cathedral Spire. Super fun route — I loved the hueco-hero-jug "roof"!

Another trip up Serenity-Sons!

Parking lot mogging.

Mr. Natural — I thought it was worth the hype! Harder than the Exasperator, but still 5.10! Not in the shade until maybe 3pm.

Finished off Jack's week on the East Buttress of El Capitan. I thought the route was alright — kinda slick and thuggy with sparse pro. Not my favorite vibe! One of my main takeaways from the trip was that I prefer more modern routes and movement. Still, it's cool to climb classics — and we made some friends along the way!


Week 3

Long story short, my friend Kevin and I set out to climb the Nose the next day. Kevin wanted to haul ground-up to Sickle, which we did, and we spent the night there as well. The next morning, however, I was not feeling psyched on walling. I was burnt out from 2 weeks of climbing with only one rest day and I needed to recover.

Kevin is the most understanding person I know. He was obviously psyched to climb the route and wanted to keep going, yet didn't question or complain when I wanted to pull the plug. Ultimately that attitude is why I love him and why we had a great next few days free climbing together.

Two hidden creatures: a California mountain snake and Kevin working his way up G-crack!

A friggin awesome day climbing Center of the Universe with Kevin. I love Fifi Buttress! Or "little Index" according to Mikey Schaefer. So yeah. No wonder I love it. Knabbed the onsight!

The Surprise — surprise, it's hot!

The top of Lower Catheral. Biking over here was kind of rugged and cool. Nice to have full-suspension.

The infamous swimming holes above Bridalveil Falls, over Gunsight Notch. Some solitude at last!

I left the Valley with zero regrets and was beyond stoked to return to the REAL valley! My favorite valley. Just look at this place! Oh we have our fair share of tourons (and for bad reason!), but the best parts of Leavenworth remain under-the-radar, even to the most dedicated of Seattle's outdoor lovers. It's a small town, with a small community, but one that's brimming with talented athletes, wild places, and countless ways to find solitude, and great climbing, along a river.
