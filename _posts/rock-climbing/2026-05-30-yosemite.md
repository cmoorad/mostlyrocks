My second season in Yosemite was...interesting! I stayed in Camp 4 for three weeks and had a different climbing partner each week. I packed a lot of climbing in, which I loved. However the vibes in the Valley ate at me. The tourons, while easy to avoid once you're actually on a route, were a part of my existence <i>every day</i>. For me climbing is about the whole experience. Your partner. The setting. The movement. The protection. The weather. The friction. Etc. Yosemite, with all the tourists, climbers, Starbucks, and...well, again, the freaking tourists (!) is just not my ideal rock climbing experience.

Many of the veterans know this. They park their vans at El Cap and grocery shop in Mariposa. They exist almost completely apart from the tourists. Meanwhile, living in Camp 4 in 2026, you simply cannot escape the madness.

Ultimately the vibe I love best for rock climbing is what led me to live in Leavenworth; abundant solitude and true wilderness. Every day at home I choose my own scene, and I love days bouldering with friends just as much as days rope soloing at Index. Everyone's different, but I originally left California for a reason and I am not in a hurry to go back to the inescapable crowds. I think my future climbing trips will trend more towards crowd avoidance. The Fall Yosemite season, I hear, might be more my speed.

<u>Climbs</u>
This year I climbed a bunch of moderate classics like the Nutcracker, Kor Beck, Absolutely Free, Central Pillar, Serenity Sons, Mr Natural, Bishops Terrace, and The Surprise. I also did some wall climbing — Skull Queen (in a day), East Buttress of El Cap (not <i>technically</i> a wall 😉), and bailing off the Nose after hauling to / sleeping on Sickle (more below...). And last-but-not-least I got on some really great single pitch and modern routes — Voyager, Yin Yang, Crack-A-Go-Go, Mack Daddy, Center of the Universe. A very diverse, informative, and exhausting trip!
